Enjoy NPB
like a local Food, cheers & etiquette

Going to a Japanese ballpark is a full cultural experience. Here's how to make the most of it — at the stadium or from home.

Stadium food

Eat like a fan — 球場グルメ

01

Takoyaki たこ焼き

¥500–

Crispy octopus balls — the classic ballpark snack, sold at nearly every stadium.

02

Yakitori 焼き鳥

¥400–

Grilled chicken skewers with tare sauce. Perfect for sharing between innings.

03

Stadium Curry カレー

¥800–

Each park has its own signature curry — some are almost as famous as the teams.

04

Beer ビール

¥700–

Sold by roaming vendors (uriko) who dash through the aisles — order with a wave and a shout.

05

Ramen ラーメン

¥900–

Night games and noodles are a perfect match, especially in the cooler months.

06

Soft Ice Cream アイスクリーム

¥400–

The unofficial dessert of Japanese baseball. Flavor of the day varies by stadium.

Pro tip: bring cash. Many stadium stands and vendors don't take cards.

02

Cheering culture

The show behind the game — 応援文化

01

Ōendan 応援団

Cheer squads

Organized fan clubs lead drum-and-brass performances and choreographed chants for every at-bat. Each team has its own fight song.

02

Lucky 7 ラッキー7

7th inning

In the 7th inning, fans sing, wave towels and join the rally — NPB's answer to the seventh-inning stretch, but louder.

03

Balloons & Towels

Free goods

Fans release team-colored balloons and toss free hot towels into the crowd during big moments. Yes, you can keep them.

04

Team Colors

Wear it

Wear the team's colors — or buy a cheap towel or umbrella at the park — and you'll instantly fit right in with the crowd.

03

Etiquette for visitors

Good to know — マナー

Do I need to speak Japanese to enjoy a game?

No. The atmosphere is universal — and scoreboards, apps and team websites increasingly include English. Many fans say the cheers speak for themselves.

How do I get tickets?

Official team websites sell online tickets (some in English). In Japan, convenience-store ticket machines also sell them. Games often have open seats — even big games are affordable compared to US MLB prices.

Can I catch a foul ball?

Yes — and it's exciting! But please don't fight over balls or throw them back onto the field. Keep it friendly.

What should I wear or bring?

Dress for the season (stadiums get hot in summer, chilly at night in spring and autumn). Small bags only, cash for food, and check each venue's rules before you go.

Why did the game end in a tie?

A regular-season NPB game can end in a draw after 12 innings — a unique quirk that keeps everyone on their feet until the very end.
