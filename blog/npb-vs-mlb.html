NPB vs
MLB Seven key differences

Rules June 2026 5 min read By NPB Guide Editorial

Same game, different world. For American fans, NPB feels familiar within minutes — then the differences start to matter.

The seven differences

  • Ties exist. Regular-season games can end in a draw after 12 innings — a concept that still shocks MLB fans.
  • Half the league has a DH. The Pacific League uses the designated hitter; the Central League sends pitchers to bat. Interleague games use the host league's rule.
  • Smaller parks. Many NPB stadiums are cozy, with quirky dimensions that reward contact and speed.
  • A different ball. NPB uses a single ball with lower, flatter seams than MLB's, and a tackier surface.
  • The playoff twist. The Climax Series gives the pennant winner a built-in head start — two games in 2026 under new conditions.
  • The atmosphere. Ōendan cheering and fight songs make the noise feel like a festival.
  • The calendar. 143 games, late March to November, with the Japan Series closing the season.
MLB is the sport's hometown. NPB is its other hometown.

Player movement

The flow between leagues runs both ways: Ichiro Suzuki became a global icon in MLB, and NPB continues to produce MLB-caliber players. The posting system — which sent Ichiro, Matsuzaka, Darvish, Tanaka and Ohtani to MLB — has not been used since the 2017–18 offseason. Watching NPB is watching a different game with the same fundamentals and a deeper history.

Why it matters

Understanding the differences makes both leagues richer. If you love the strategy of pitcher-batting lineups, the Central League is your home; if you prefer designated-hitter fireworks, the Pacific League awaits.
