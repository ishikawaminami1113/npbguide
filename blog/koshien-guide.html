Koshien
stadium The cathedral of baseball

Stadiums April 2026 6 min read By NPB Guide Editorial

Koshien Stadium is where Japanese baseball goes to remember itself. Home of the Hanshin Tigers — and of the national high-school championships — it is the most famous ballpark in Japan.

Why Koshien is special

Built in 1924, Koshien hosts two national obsessions: the Tigers' home games and the spring/summer high-school tournaments that unite the whole country in front of the TV. The stadium's dirt is famously treated with reverence — players and fans talk about the "sacred soil" of Koshien.

Getting there

Koshien sits in Nishinomiya, Hyogo, between Osaka and Kobe. Take the Hanshin Railway to Koshien Station — the stadium is a short walk from the exit. On game days, the walk itself becomes a festival of yellow-and-black flags and food stalls.

Where to sit

For the full experience, aim for the outfield stands, where the ōendan drums echo and the cheering sections create a wall of sound. Seated infield options are calmer — ideal for first-timers who want to follow the game itself. Buy tickets early for big games; see our ticket guide.

At Koshien, even the air feels like it remembers every game.

What to eat

Koshien's concessions are legendary: curry, yakitori and the classic ballpark snacks. Come hungry and with cash. For the full ranking, see the best stadium foods in NPB.

One more thing

If you visit in summer, the high-school tournament is in full swing — even non-fans find it gripping. Either way, Koshien is the single best place to understand why baseball means so much to Japan.
