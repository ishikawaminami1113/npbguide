The DH
debate One rule, two leagues

Rules July 2026 4 min read By NPB Guide Editorial

In 2026, NPB remains one of the last major leagues split by a single rule: the designated hitter. The Pacific League has it; the Central League doesn't. Here's why that divide still matters.

The split

The Pacific League adopted the DH decades ago, building lineups around power bats that never take the field. The Central League keeps the classic game — pitchers bat, double switches matter, and managing a nine-player lineup is an art form.

The June compromise

During interleague play in June, every team plays across the divide — and the rule follows the home park: DH in Pacific parks, pitcher-batting in Central parks. It's a built-in experiment in how much the rule changes a game.

A pitcher who can hit is a Central League treasure — and a Pacific League oddity.

Why the debate endures

Fans argue about identity, not just strategy: the Central League sells tradition, the Pacific League sells offense. The split even shapes the Japan Series, where the rule alternates by park and managers on both sides plan around it.

What it means for viewers

Love small-ball and live pitching? Follow the Central League. Prefer slugfests and DH fireworks? The Pacific League is waiting. See the NPB vs MLB explainer for how this compares to America's unified DH rule.
