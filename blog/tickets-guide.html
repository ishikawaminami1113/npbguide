Ticket
guide Getting in is the easy part

Practical June 2026 5 min read By NPB Guide Editorial

Good news: NPB tickets are among the most affordable in pro sports, and the buying process is straightforward once you know the three main channels.

Where to buy

  • Official team websites — the primary source; some offer English pages or English-friendly checkout.
  • Convenience-store ticket machines — a very Japanese option: walk up, pick a game, pay cash, print the ticket.
  • Day-of-game counters and secondary sellers — many parks sell same-day tickets, and games often have open seats.

What to expect to pay

Most seats run roughly ¥1,500–5,000 (about $10–35). Premium and box seats go higher, but a great night out — ticket, food, beer — often lands under ¥8,000 for one person. Compared with MLB prices, it's a bargain.

Baseball in Japan: where the atmosphere is priceless and the ticket is not.

Seated or standing?

Outfield standing areas put you inside the cheering culture — drums, songs, balloons — and are the classic choice for young fans. Reserved seats give you a fixed view and a calmer experience. First-timers: pick reserved infield first, then graduate to the outfield.

Tips for visitors

Buy in advance for weekend games and rivalry matchups (Giants–Tigers sells out fast). Bring cash, arrive 30–45 minutes early for the pre-game atmosphere, and check each venue's bag policy before you go. Japan Series 2026: the Lawson first lottery runs October 13–15, the advance lottery October 14–17, and general sale opens October 22 at noon.
