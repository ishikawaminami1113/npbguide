The WBC
and NPB Japan takes on the world

International August 2026 5 min read By NPB Guide Editorial

Every four years, the World Baseball Classic puts Japan's pros on the world stage — and shows why NPB is one of the sport's great talent pools.

Why the WBC matters

The WBC is the only tournament where the world's best players compete for national teams — NPB players alongside MLB players. For Japanese fans, it's the sport's biggest stage; for the world, it's the clearest window into how strong Japan's players really are.

It is also a stage that has cost Japan recently. The 2026 tournament ran from March 5 to 17 across four cities — Tokyo, San Juan, Houston and Miami. Samurai Japan went a perfect 4–0 in its Tokyo pool at Tokyo Dome, then lost 8–5 to Venezuela in the quarterfinals at loanDepot Park — the earliest exit in Japan's WBC history, two years after winning the 2023 title. Venezuela went on to win the tournament for the first time.

The Japanese pipeline

NPB develops players for the global stage. Ichiro Suzuki came through Japan's system and became an MLB legend, and NPB-trained stars now populate rosters across both leagues. National-team games also sell out instantly; the Samurai Japan brand is a cultural force at home and abroad.

The WBC is where NPB's heart beats for the whole world to hear.

What to watch

Follow the Samurai Japan roster announcements, the pool play drama, and the knockout rounds. Even if you can't watch live, highlights move fast — see our watch guide for legal options and time-zone math.

The bigger picture

The WBC grows the game everywhere — and makes NPB part of the conversation wherever baseball is discussed. For international fans, it's the easiest entry point: one tournament, national pride, and the world's best of both leagues.
