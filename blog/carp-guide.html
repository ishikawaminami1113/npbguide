Hiroshima
Toyo Carp The people's club

Teams Updated October 2026 5 min read By NPB Guide Editorial

The Hiroshima Toyo Carp are more than a baseball team: they are a symbol of recovery for an entire region, and one of the most beloved underdog stories in the sport.

A club of the region

Founded in 1949, the Carp represent Hiroshima and the wider Chugoku region — an area with no big-city media machine of its own. When the Carp win, the region celebrates; when they lose, the red flags still fly. The club's titles (1975, 1979, 1980, 1984) are etched into local memory.

Loyalty as identity

The Carp are famous for a tradition of loyalty that stands out in a sport of frequent transfers. Fans reward that faith with some of the most devoted home support in NPB at Mazda Zoom-Zoom Stadium, where the atmosphere is warm, loud and deeply local.

In Hiroshima, the Carp don't just play baseball. They carry the city.

The underdog spirit

Unlike the media giants of Tokyo and Osaka, the Carp survive on regional pride and grit. That's part of their charm: supporting the Carp means supporting a community, not a brand. For international fans, the club offers a window into how deeply baseball is rooted outside Japan's biggest cities.

How to follow

Home games are easy to combine with a visit to the Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park — a powerful pairing of sport and history. See the ticket guide and the NPB vs MLB explainer for context.

2026: a rebuilding year

The Carp finished fourth (61–78–4), 17 games back — a quiet, rebuilding season. But the red flags still fly over Mazda Zoom-Zoom Stadium, and in Hiroshima, loyalty through the lean years is the whole point. The region will be counting on a stronger 2027.
