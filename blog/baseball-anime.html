Baseball
Anime Where to start

Culture Updated October 2026 6 min read By NPB Guide Editorial

If you love baseball and anime, this is the overlap you didn't know you needed. Japanese baseball manga and anime have been running for decades — and they're one of the best ways to understand the sport before you ever set foot in a stadium.

Why baseball anime teaches you the game

NPB's culture — high school tournaments at Koshien, the respect for the pitcher-catcher battery, the emotional weight of a single at-bat — is easier to grasp after watching a season of anime than after reading a rulebook. Series like Ace of Diamond and Big Windup! are celebrated for their technical accuracy: mechanics, defensive positioning, sign systems, and the psychology of a lineup. You'll walk into an NPB park already knowing why the crowd reacts the way it does.

The big three

Major (manga from 1994, anime 2004–2010, six seasons) follows Gorou Honda from little league through high school to the pros — the classic coming-of-age sports saga, and the most universally watched entry point. Ace of Diamond (anime 2013–2016, followed by Act II from 2019) follows first-year pitcher Eijun Sawamura at powerhouse Seidou High and the battle to reach Koshien. And Big Windup! (Ookiku Furikabutte, anime from 2007) is the technical deep-dive — anxious pitcher Ren Mihashi and his catcher Takaya Abe rebuilding a small high-school program.

For the grown-ups

One Outs (manga 1998–2006, anime 2008) flips the script: Toua Tokuchi is a pitcher who earns 5 million yen for every out he records and pays a penalty for every run — a psychological, adult-oriented take on the professional game. Cross Game (manga 2005–2010, anime 2009–2010) blends romance and grief with baseball, and won the 54th Shogakukan Manga Award. And Gurazeni looks at pro ball through contracts, salary and career longevity — the side of the sport fans rarely see.

The classics

Touch and H2 come from Mitsuru Adachi, the master of baseball manga — both are essential reading in Japan. Rookies is the beloved story of a slacker class that discovers team spirit. Together, they trace how generations of Japanese kids fell in love with the game.

Anime doesn't just reflect Japanese baseball culture — for many fans, it created it.

Then go see the real thing

Once you've watched a season or two, come see it live. Our 12-team directory explains who's who, our cheering guide covers the chants, and the game-day guide handles tickets, food and what to expect. Start with our NPB primer and you'll be fluent in no time.
